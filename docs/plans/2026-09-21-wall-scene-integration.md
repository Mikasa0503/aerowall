# Wall scene and contact integration

This is the next physical integration layer, not the reward-bearing WallRally environment. It subclasses fixed SingleJuggle only to construct the original Air, ball, ground and clone groups plus a per-environment fixed cuboid wall. Wall center [2.5, 0, 3] m, dimensions [0.2, 4, 6] m and material restitution 0.8 are fixture choices, not the frozen task's reachable target set or effective elasticity range.

All existing Air collision shapes are explicitly enabled before PhysX parses the stage. The pinned USD files are not modified. Collider owners are discovered from the authored scene and must have exactly one enabled collision shape each; otherwise construction fails. This makes actor-pair GPU buffers unambiguous for the current asset rather than silently merging multiple shape identities.

Ball sensors filter wall, ground, bat and every remaining enabled body. Body sensors filter wall and ground. PhysX FOUND/PERSIST/LOST headers identify the lifecycle and GPU contact buffers provide valid geometry/impulses. All positive bat manifold points must classify as top-cap points; a manifold that includes a side/bottom point is illegal. Each wall point must lie on the intended front face before it is target-eligible. Cross-environment contacts, unmatched positive GPU samples, invalid vectors and unregistered ball pairs are surfaced as integration failures.

Seven independently initialized fixtures exercise cap, non-cap, ball/body, ball/wall, ball/ground, drone/wall and drone/ground contact in 16 environments. Initialization places bodies and velocities; only PhysX changes trajectories during each measured window. A separating warm-up step flushes prior fixtures before resetting ledgers. These trials test routing, not a controller or learned wall-return skill. Actual episode resets in the future task must separately guarantee valid manifold lifecycle behavior.

The wall contact reader's per-environment views are a correctness implementation; 512-environment throughput has not been measured. Remaining task integration includes substep capture, actor observations without hidden elasticity, target publication, physical bounds and numerical failure handling, independent reset/controller/history tests, and rewards after the remaining action-analysis gate.

## Native findings and project-stage repair under validation

The as-authored Air asset has a 0.12 m tall body box and a 0.11 m tall bat cylinder, both centered at the same height. The body top is 5 mm above the bat top. Original body collision is disabled. With body collision enabled, the centered cap fixture actually hits the body (16/16), while all six other routed fixture types pass. Preserving this failure is essential: marking these body impulses as legal cap impacts would violate the task.

Options: leave body collision disabled (violates the illegal-body-contact requirement); shrink the body collider without an asset-based target (changes body coverage); translate the bat collider so its top coincides with the already-authored visual bat top (a small, explicit asset-based stage override). The last option is the candidate tested here. The visual mesh's z center is 0.08 m and thickness 0.006 m, so its top is 0.083 m. Moving the unchanged radius-0.05/height-0.11 cylinder upward by 0.028 m places its cap at that height. This is not a change to the pinned USD or the original development checkpoint, and it is not enabled by default. Bat mass, auto COM and inertia are recorded; previous physics/policy gates do not automatically transfer to this body-enabled geometry.

GPU contact coefficients can be signed. One body contact returns coefficient -0.1503064 and normal approximately -z, whose product equals the +z aggregate impulse and the ball's gravity-corrected momentum change. The reader now retains the raw signed coefficient and canonicalizes magnitude/direction only after checking that the sum of per-point vectors matches the native pair aggregate.

CPU-narrowphase cylinder contacts can have valid CPU points while GPU buffers lack their impulses. Conversely, box contacts may require GPU readback. The router selects valid current CPU points when present and otherwise qualifies GPU points with the current PhysX pair header. GPU buffers can retain departed ground contacts after a teleport; a positive buffer without a current header is recorded and ignored, never converted into an event. In the as-authored seven-fixture run 38 such unmatched slots are observed. Reset/history correctness remains a live-environment gate, not just a dictionary-clear assertion.
